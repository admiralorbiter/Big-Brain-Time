Functional Requirements

Functional requirements specify observable behaviors that the software is to provide — specifically policies to be enforced and processes to be carried out.

Observable Behavior BDD Scenarios ATDD Acceptance

1.4 Functional Requirements

Canonical SWEBOK Text

Functional requirements specify observable behaviors:

  • Banking Policy Examples: "An account shall always have at least one customer as its owner," and "the balance of an account shall never be negative."
  • Banking Process Examples: Depositing money, withdrawing money, transferring money between accounts.
  • Technical Protocol Examples (TCP/IP): "A Port shall exist with 0, 1, or many Connections, but a Connection shall exist on exactly one Port," connection states (LISTEN, SYN_SENT, ESTABLISHED, CLOSING), and TTL segment expiration handling.

The Perfect Technology Filter Test

How do you prove a requirement is functional vs nonfunctional? SWEBOK cites McMenamin & Palmer's Perfect Technology Filter test:

"Functional requirements are those that would still need to be stated even if a computer with infinite speed, unlimited memory, zero cost, and zero failures existed on which to construct the software."

⚡ Quick Reference Card EARS & SbE

Controlled Natural Language & Specification by Example

Combining EARS (Easy Approach to Requirements Syntax) controlled templates with Specification by Example (SbE) and decision tables to eliminate prose ambiguity without requiring formal mathematical notation.

9.4 Controlled Natural Language & SbE

EARS 5 Syntax Patterns
1. Ubiquitous: The <system name> shall <system response>
2. Event-driven: WHEN <trigger>, the <system name> shall <system response>
3. State-driven: WHILE <state>, the <system name> shall <system response>
4. Option-driven: WHERE <feature is included>, the <system name> shall <system response>
5. Complex: WHILE <state>, WHEN <trigger>, the <system name> shall <system response>

Specification by Example (SbE)

Treating concrete scenarios and decision tables as living documentation created through collaborative discovery between business stakeholders, developers, and testers.

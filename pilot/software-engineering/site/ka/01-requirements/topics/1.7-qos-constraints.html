Quality of Service (QoS) Constraints

Requirements that do not mandate specific named technologies, but instead specify acceptable performance levels (response time, throughput, accuracy, reliability, availability, security, scalability).

QoS Constraints Nonfunctional Reqs

1.7 Quality of Service Constraints

Canonical SWEBOK Text

QoS constraints specify acceptable performance levels an automated solution must exhibit:

  • Response time, throughput, accuracy, reliability, availability, scalability.
  • Reference Standard: ISO/IEC 25010 (SQuaRE - System and software quality models).
  • Safety and Security are particularly critical QoS domains where requirements are frequently overlooked.

Mutual Support vs Negative Conflict

QoS constraints interact with each other in complex ways:

Mutually Supporting

Improving modifiability often improves reliability, as both stem from clean, decoupled code structure.

Conflicting / Trade-off

Extreme execution speed optimizations often reduce code modifiability by introducing complex micro-optimizations.

2.2 Elicitation Techniques

Canonical SWEBOK Text

Elicitation is not a passive activity. Many product requirements are tacit or hidden in uncollected workflow data.

Stakeholder Elicitation Techniques:

  • Interviews & Focus Groups: Structured, semi-structured, and open-ended.
  • Facilitated Workshops: Joint Application Development (JAD) and Joint Requirements Planning (JRP).
  • Exploratory Prototyping: Low-fidelity & high-fidelity user interface prototypes.
  • User Story Mapping: Visual organization of capabilities into epics, features, and stories.

Observation & Tacit Knowledge Discovery:

  • Observation: Studying stakeholders in their actual working environment.
  • Apprenticing: Software engineers learning by performing the work themselves.
  • Quality Function Deployment (QFD): Using the House of Quality matrix to map customer wants to engineering characteristics.
  • Defect Tracking Mining: Analyzing defect databases of previous system iterations.

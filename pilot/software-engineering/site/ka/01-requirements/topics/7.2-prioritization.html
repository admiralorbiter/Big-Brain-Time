Requirements Prioritization & The Kano Model

Focuses software engineers on delivering high-value functionality first. The Kano Model proves that prioritizing solely by positive satisfaction leads to errors — developers must weigh both satisfaction from presence and dissatisfaction from absence.

7.2 Requirements Prioritization

Canonical SWEBOK Text

Factors determining requirement priority:

  • Value, desirability, and user satisfaction;
  • Undesirability & user dissatisfaction (Kano Model);
  • Cost to deliver and maintain over service life;
  • Technical implementation risk & non-adoption risk.

The Kano Model Example (Email Client)

Users express higher positive satisfaction for an effective spam filter than for handling attachments. However, lack of attachment support causes extreme dissatisfaction. Weighing both criteria correctly ranks handling attachments as higher priority than the spam filter.

Objective Priority Formula

Priority = Cost / (Value * (1 - Risk))

Model-Based Requirements Specification

Using visual modeling languages (UML, SysML) to specify requirements in a technology-free manner. Divided into Structural Models (policies to enforce) and Behavioral Models (processes to carry out).

4.4 Model-Based Requirements Specification

Canonical SWEBOK Text

Requirements models fall into 2 general categories:

1. Structural Models

Specify policies to be enforced. Logical class models, conceptual data models, entity-relationship diagrams (ERDs).

2. Behavioral Models

Specify processes to be executed. Use case models, interaction diagrams, UML statecharts, activity diagrams, data-flow diagrams.

Spectrum of Formality

Varies from Agile Modeling (sketches focused on communication over form), to Semiformal Modeling (UML with defined semantics), to Formal Modeling (Z, VDM, SDL enabling mechanical proof analysis).

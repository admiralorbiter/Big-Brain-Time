Why Categorize Requirements This Way?

Categorization separates stakeholder expertise (business domain experts validate policy; software engineers validate technology constraints), manages complex divide-and-conquer activities, and guides elicitation, analysis, and specification choices.

1.8 Why Categorize Requirements This Way?

Canonical SWEBOK Text

Categorization provides 6 lifecycle benefits:

  1. Requirements in distinct categories come from different sources;
  2. Elicitation techniques vary by source;
  3. Analysis techniques vary by category;
  4. Specification techniques vary by category;
  5. Validation authorities vary by category;
  6. Categories affect the resulting software architecture in different ways.

Divide-and-Conquer Complexity Management

Software engineers can address policy and process complexities without worrying about automation technology constraints simultaneously (and vice versa). Non-technical domain experts reviewing functional specifications will not give up in frustration over technology details they do not care about.
